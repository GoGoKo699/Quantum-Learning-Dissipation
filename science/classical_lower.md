# Why a classical controller loses a squared logarithm

[Model](MODEL.md) · [Results](results.md) · [Upper construction](learner.md)

## 1. A history is an effect on the consumed copies

For the converse, draw the unknown pure state P from Haar measure. Fix a time t and put m = t-1. Any classical record H available before the next specimen is generated by a quantum instrument acting on the first m copies, with independent ancillas and randomness absorbed into the instrument. Therefore, for every nonzero-probability record h, there is a positive effect E_h such that

```math
\Pr(H=h\mid P)=\mathrm{Tr}(E_h P^{\otimes m}).
```

The argument permits a collective measurement of those m copies as a relaxation, even though the original device cannot retain them. Coarsening or overwriting a history merely replaces the effect by a positive sum. The reduction therefore covers arbitrary adaptive classical controllers, not only the projective measurements used by the upper construction.

## 2. The inherited posterior identity

Let Pi_m be the projector onto the symmetric subspace of m qubits and let A_h = Pi_m E_h Pi_m. For m at least one, set

```math
\tau_h=\mathrm{Tr}_{2,\ldots,m}\frac{A_h}{\mathrm{Tr}A_h}.
```

The Haar-posterior identity is

```math
\rho_h=\mathbb E[P\mid h]=\frac{I+m\tau_h}{m+2}.
```

This arbitrary-effect identity and its outcome-wise entropy bound are inherited from Hayashi, Dasgupta, and Warsi, Lemma 10 and its entropy optimization; see [sources](../references/PRIORITY.md). Here is a self-contained derivation. Haar integration gives integral P^(tensor m) dP = Pi_m/(m+1). In the numerator of the posterior, use

```math
\Pi_{m+1}=\frac1{m+1}\left(I+\sum_{i=1}^m S_{i,m+1}\right)(\Pi_m\otimes I).
```

Taking the partial trace against A_h yields (Tr A_h)(I+m tau_h)/((m+1)(m+2)). Dividing by the outcome probability Tr A_h/(m+1) proves the formula. At m=0, the posterior is I/2 directly.

Since tau_h is a density operator, both eigenvalues of rho_h lie between 1/(m+2) and (m+1)/(m+2). Binary entropy is minimized at the endpoints of this interval, hence

```math
S(\rho_h)\ge h\!\left(\frac1{m+2}\right)
=h\!\left(\frac1{t+1}\right).
```

This is a conditional, outcome-wise statement. No assumption that an estimate is unbiased or that the outcome has appreciable probability is needed.

## 3. The controller entropy must telescope

Let M_(t-1) denote the entire persistent classical controller before round t. Under the Haar experiment, the incoming accessible state has entropy

```math
S(M_{t-1}S_t)
=H(M_{t-1})+\sum_h p_h S(\rho_h).
```

At the end of the round, the outgoing specimen O_t has dimension two, while the retained controller is M_t. Subadditivity gives

```math
S(M_t O_t)\le H(M_t)+\ln2.
```

Apply the Gibbs entropy balance from the model to this whole accessible transformation. The expected extracted work satisfies

```math
\beta\mathbb E W_t
\le\ln2-\mathbb E_h S(\rho_h)
+H(M_t)-H(M_{t-1}).
```

All temporary ancillas have either been returned or included in M_t. A bath component that could be reused to retrieve input information would instead be persistent memory and cannot be omitted. Correlations with inaccessible previous outputs do not invalidate the local Gibbs identity: the fresh bath is independent of the current accessible marginal, to which the entropy balance is applied.

Finally restoring M_N to its initial state costs at least its entropy increase, so

```math
\beta\mathbb E W_{\rm restore}\le H(M_0)-H(M_N).
```

Summing cancels every controller entropy term, including any initially mixed random seed. Thus

```math
\beta\mathbb E W_{\rm net}
\le N\ln2-\sum_{t=1}^N\mathbb E_h S(\rho_h)
\le N\ln2-B_N.
```

This proves the Haar-average lower bound; the minimax lower bound follows because a supremum is at least its Haar average. The bound is uniform in finite controller dimension, so it survives limits of increasing finite memories and finite-outcome discretizations.

## 4. Size of the lower bound

Direct expansion gives

```math
h\!\left(\frac1{t+1}\right)
=\ln(t+1)-\ln t+\frac{\ln t}{t+1}.
```

Summing the first difference telescopes. The difference between ln(t)/(t+1) and ln(t)/t is absolutely summable, and integral comparison of the latter gives one half of (ln N)^2 plus a bounded correction. This proves the expansion in the results page.

## 5. The separate probe-regret consequence

Before a probe is chosen, the same posterior identity bounds its mean overlap by the largest eigenvalue of rho_h, at most t/(t+1). Independent randomization of the probe does not increase that maximum. Hence the Haar-average expected infidelity of any causal rank-one probe in round t is at least 1/(t+1). Summation gives H_(N+1)-1. This is a learning bound; the thermodynamic bound is stronger because it charges an entropy, not just an infidelity.
